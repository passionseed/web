# PS-01 "Day 7" camp shirt: spec v1

Picked by a 3-lens review (garment designer, brand fit, production) of 8 AI-generated directions in `gen/`, then pressure-tested by a skeptic. Base garment: `gen/codex/codex-3-night-shift-full.png`. Back-art concept grafted from `gen/agy/agy-1-sashiko-chore-full.png`, redrawn as original art.

## Ranking

## Garment
PS-01 'Day 7' camp shirt. A boxy, short-sleeve, open-collar (one-piece convertible camp collar) shirt for the team, mentors and community, unisex grading S to XL. Silhouette: wide and slightly cropped, dropped shoulder about 5cm, straight hem with side vents, no back pleat (the back panel must stay flat for the print). Finished measurements for size M, as half-chest / back length from HPS / shoulder / sleeve length from shoulder point / half sleeve opening: 62 / 70 / 56 / 23 / 20.5cm. S 59 / 68 / 53 / 22 / 19.5. L 65 / 72 / 59 / 24 / 21.5. XL 68 / 74 / 62 / 25 / 22.5. Run of 10 plus 1 sample. Set the size split by having all 10 people try on the sample; expect roughly S2 M4 L3 XL1. The look is a quiet front (pocket, hand stitch, a small patch at the hem) and one strong back panel, the way an independent label builds a shirt, not a team-merch tee.

## Fabric and colour
Shell: linen-cotton slub plain weave, about 55% linen and 45% cotton, 170 to 190 gsm, 145 to 150cm wide, reactive piece-dyed (reactive dye is needed if you print with discharge), enzyme or softener washed. Colour: deep burgundy/oxblood, target #5E1B2C, near Pantone 19-1617 TCX Burgundy. Match it to the dark end of the logo disc; reject anything purple-magenta or brick-red. Buy about 22m (1.7m per shirt x 11, plus 2m for print and wash tests). Before cutting, wash the whole roll at 40C and tumble dry, then re-measure: linen-cotton shrinks 3 to 5%, and the print goes on before sewing, so shrinkage must happen first. Print ink: one colour, bone/cream #EDE3CC (near Pantone 7499 C). Hand-stitch threads: natural off-white sashiko cotton, and one skein of flame-yellow sashiko cotton matched to the logo yellow #FFC23F. Construction thread: tonal burgundy. Fallback colourway, only if no reactive-dyed burgundy linen-cotton is in stock: undyed natural/ecru linen-cotton with the same art in black water-based ink, which is literally the photocopy look of the reference. Do not substitute polyester-linen blends; they trap heat.

## Construction
Collar: one-piece open camp collar with a thread or self-fabric button loop at the top of the left front. Fronts: fold-back self facing 6cm, 5 buttonholes, buttonholes horizontal on the right front. Back: double-layer back yoke (self outer, self inner, seams enclosed), with a single flat back panel below it that carries the print. Seams: French seams on side and underarm seams (linen frays), armholes flat-felled or overlocked then topstitched at 6mm, all at 12 stitches per inch with tonal thread. Hem: 1.5cm double-turn, straight all round (front and back the same length), with 8cm side vents bar-tacked at the top. Sleeves: 3cm double-turn hem, topstitched. Chest pocket on the wearer's left: finished 12cm wide x 14cm tall, 2.5cm folded mouth, bottom corners clipped at 1.5cm (as in the codex-3 image), top edge 22cm below HPS for M and 3cm off the front edge. A vertical stitch line 3cm in from the centre-front side of the pocket makes a pen slot. Order of work: (1) pre-wash fabric, (2) cut, (3) screen print the back panel flat and cure it, (4) hand-stitch the pocket piece before attaching it, so the knots hide inside the pocket, (5) sew. Labels: woven main label centred inside the back neck, sewn into the yoke seam at both ends, with the size tab beside it. Edition tag folded into the left side seam, 2cm above the hem. Hem patch on the wearer's left front, 3cm above the hem edge and 4cm in from the vent edge, machine edge-stitched 1.5mm in with matching thread.

## Artwork to make
### 00 Logo master vector (source for every other file)
- Placement: Not printed. Every derived artwork is built from this file.
- Size: Any; 100mm master artboard
- Technique: Vector redraw
- Artwork: logo-master.svg: a faithful vector redraw of /Users/bunyasit/dev/passionseed/web/output/merch/ps-01/gen/refs/logo.png in separate named layers: disc (maroon; flatten the gradient to #6B1A35 for embroidery), flame base (#FFC23F), orange tongues (#FF8A1E), bright yellow tongues (#FFE11F), seed (black), eye whites, pupils, brows (white). The top-right flame tongue must break the disc outline as it does in the original. Check the overlay against the PNG at 100% before deriving anything else. No smile line or extra shapes: the AI images invented a mouth inside the flame.

### 01 Back panel: 'Day 7' comic panel + threshold mascot + bitmap wordmark (one screen)
- Placement: Back panel, centred on the centre-back line, top of the artwork 5cm below the back-yoke seam (about 15 to 16cm below the collar seam on M). One size for all sizes S to XL.
- Size: Overall artwork box 26 W x 30 H. Comic panel border 24 x 20; mascot about 19 x 19 inside the panel, with the top-right flame tongue rising 2.5cm above the panel edge; wordmark 25 W x about 4.4 cap height, 1.5cm below the panel.
- Technique: One-colour screen print on the flat cut back panel before sewing. First choice: cream colour-discharge (soft hand, breathes), if a 20cm strike-off on the actual fabric discharges cleanly. Fallback: water-based high-opacity cream, two strokes with a flash between. 110 to 125 mesh (43 to 48 t/cm), 70 durometer squeegee. Cure 160 to 170C for 2 to 3 min in a conveyor dryer, or a heat press at 160C for 90s through baking paper. Tell the printer the film is already 1-bit bitmap: output it as-is and do not apply halftone screening in the RIP.
- Artwork: back-day7-600ppi.png (1-bit, 6142 x 7087 px = 26 x 30cm at 600 ppi), black = ink, plus a PDF proof at 100% for a paper test pinned on a shirt. Built in code from four layers, then merged and cleaned. (a) MASCOT: from logo-master.svg, build a grayscale map in which the flame base = 85% ink, bright yellow tongues = 100%, orange tongues = 45% (so they come out as a grainy mid-tone), eye whites = 100%, brows = 100%, and seed, pupils and disc = 0%, so the burgundy fabric shows through as the dark seed and the disc is dropped entirely. Add a 1-cell no-ink keyline around both eye whites where they touch the flame, so they do not merge. Add photocopy grain (gaussian noise about +/-18%, 1px blur), then a 50% threshold on a 20 px/inch grid (1.27mm cells, about 150 cells wide), upscaled nearest-neighbour. No smoothing. (b) PANEL BORDER: hand-inked on paper with a brush pen, a slightly wobbly rectangle with a 2.5 to 3.5mm line, broken at top-right where the flame tongue crosses it, with 3 short motion ticks at the break. Inside the top-left corner, a small comic caption box 4.5 x 1.6cm reading 'DAY 7' in the bitmap serif from (c) at a 1.2cm cap height. Outside the panel at bottom-right, a hand-scratched tally of 7 marks (one 4-plus-slash gate plus 2 strokes), about 3cm wide. Scan at 600 ppi and threshold at 50% so the toner edge stays. (c) WORDMARK 'PassionSeed' (exact spelling and case, one line, never split): set in DM Serif Display or Libre Caslon Text Bold (both OFL), rasterise at a 20 px cap height with anti-aliasing off and a 50% threshold, then hand-fix pixels so every hairline is at least 1 cell and the S, e and d read clearly. Upscale nearest-neighbour to a 2.2mm cell (cap height about 4.4cm). (d) CLEANUP: remove ink islands and close holes smaller than 0.5mm (12 px at 600 ppi) so the screen holds them, and keep total ink coverage of the 26 x 30 box at 35 to 45%. No sparkles, comet, hearts or running figure.

### 02 Hem logo patch
- Placement: Wearer's left front, 3cm above the hem, 4cm in from the side-vent edge (same side as the pocket)
- Size: 5.0 tall x about 4.6 wide, heat-cut to the silhouette (the disc plus the flame tongue breaking out)
- Technique: Embroidered patch, laser/heat-cut edge with no merrow border (a merrow border cannot follow the flame break), on a stiff twill base with iron-on backing plus machine edge-stitch. Order 20 pieces: 11 for this run, the rest for samples and replacements.
- Artwork: patch-logo.svg plus a reference PNG at 1:1, simplified from logo-master.svg for embroidery: flat maroon disc, yellow flame fill, at most 2 orange tongues (each at least 1.5mm wide), black satin seed, white eye fills with black satin pupils (at least 2mm), white brows as a satin stroke at least 1mm. No green or other invented colours. The embroidery shop digitises it to DST at 1:1 (digitising THB 500 to 1,000). Approve a sew-out photo before the run.

### 03 Pocket hand-stitch map (sashiko accent)
- Placement: Chest pocket piece, stitched flat before the pocket is attached
- Size: Pocket finished 12 x 14; stitch lines 6mm inside the finished edge
- Technique: Hand running stitch done by the team in-house: about 30 min per pocket, roughly 6 hours for all 11 as one team session. Row A in off-white sashiko cotton along the two sides and bottom, 4mm stitch and 2mm gap, following the clipped corners. Row B in flame yellow, 10mm below the pocket-mouth fold: exactly 7 stitches of 6mm with 4mm gaps, centred (one per sprint day). A small off-white cross stitch (jujizashi) at each top pocket corner reinforces the stress point.
- Artwork: pocket-stitch-map.svg and a PDF at 100% scale, generated in code: the pocket outline including the 2.5cm fold allowance and seam allowance, with every stitch drawn as a dash and the entry and exit holes marked as dots, Row A and Row B in two colours, and a 5cm scale bar to check the print scale. Transfer to each pocket by pricking the paper and marking with a heat-erasable (Frixion) pen or chalk.

### 04 Neck label
- Placement: Inside centre back neck, sewn into the yoke seam; size tab beside it

## Utility detail
Two small, honest work details, both cheap and invisible until used: (1) The chest pocket is split by one vertical stitch line 3cm from its centre-front edge, making a pen or stylus slot. (2) A self-fabric glasses loop hangs below the pocket: a 1.2cm-wide tube, 5cm long, folded into a loop that shows about 2.5cm, bar-tacked through pocket and body at the centre of the pocket's bottom edge. This is the detail image's fabric loop, not the D-ring. The 7 flame-yellow stitches on the pocket mouth are the one story detail: one stitch per sprint day.

## Trims
5 natural coconut-shell buttons per shirt, 15mm (24L), 4-hole, sewn with an off-white thread cross; buy 70 for spares (Sampeng, about THB 3 to 6 each). One top button loop in self fabric or thread. Light fusible interfacing on the collar and front facings only (keep it soft). Embroidered heat-cut hem patch (graphic 02). Woven neck label plus size tab (graphic 04). Printed edition tag (graphic 05). Sashiko threads in off-white and flame yellow, sashiko needles, a Frixion pen. Tonal burgundy construction thread. No metal hardware, no D-rings, no snaps, no rivets, no fobs, no plastic googly eyes.

## Bangkok production
About 3 weeks (around 18 working days) for 10 pieces plus 1 sample.
Days 1 to 3: the team renders graphics 00, 01 and 03 to 05 in code; the artist hand-inks the panel border and tally, scans them and merges them in. Print file 01 at 100% on paper and pin it on a shirt to check scale and placement. In parallel, buy 1m of each candidate burgundy linen-cotton at Phahurat or Sampeng (or Bobae), wash-test it at 40C, then buy 22m of the winner and pre-wash the roll. Send graphic 02 to a Bobae or Sampeng embroidery shop for digitising and a sew-out (5 to 7 days). Order labels and tape (5 to 10 days).
Days 3 to 6: a shirt workshop in Pratunam or Bobae drafts the pattern from a reference camp shirt the team brings in, with the measurements above, and cuts and sews 1 sample. At the same time, a screen printer that does discharge burns one screen from the 1-bit film and strikes off discharge and water-based cream on swatches of the real fabric. Pick whichever is soft and even after 3 washes.
Days 6 to 8: sew the sample with a printed back panel; fit it on 2 or 3 people across sizes; wash it 3 times inside out; check print, shrinkage and the loop. Fix the pattern, and fix the size split by having all 10 people try on the sample.
Days 8 to 10: the workshop cuts 10 sets; the printer prints and cures the 10 back panels flat; the panels go back to the workshop.
Days 10 to 11: team session to hand-stitch 10 pocket pieces from the stitch map (about 30 min each).
Days 11 to 16: the workshop sews 10 shirts (French seams, vents, loop, pen slot, labels, patch, buttons).
Days 16 to 18: QC (spelling of 'PassionSeed' on print and labels, print registration, bar tacks), hand-number the edition tags, press, wash one shirt as a final check, hand out. For the run of 40, reuse the pattern, screen, patch file and labels; only fabric, CMT and printing scale.

## Skeptic review (must apply)
Verdict: The pick holds. codex-3's breathable cotton-linen camp shirt is the only body in the set that a Bangkok team can wear and that also reads as an independent-label piece, and it spells 'PassionSeed' correctly; only the detail image has gibberish text. But the rebuild spec as written would print a solid cream flame blob with lost brows and tongues, and a wordmark that cannot fit its own box. Fix the tone map, fix the wordmark size, treat the discharge strike-off as a hard gate, and prototype an illustrator-drawn back next to the logo-panel back, because the founder's 'art taste' complaint is aimed most directly at the back.

### Risks
- The tone map in the spec will turn the mascot back into the solid blob the synthesis was trying to get rid of. The flame base is set to 85% ink and the bright tongues to 100%, with grain of about +/-18% and a 50% threshold. Since 85 - 18 = 67 is still above 50, the base and the tongues both print solid, so the inner flame detail disappears. The brows are set to 100% ink, but in logo.png they sit partly over the flame (the left brow at about x290-420, y405-470), so they merge into it as well. You end up with a cream flame silhouette with a hole for the seed and eye blobs fused to it, which is the same oversized solid flame the codex-3 render was criticised for.
- The wordmark sizes contradict each other. The spec asks for a 25cm-wide 'PassionSeed' with a 4.4cm cap height. In a serif, this word is about 8 to 9 cap heights wide; codex-3's own wordmark measures about 8.2. A 4.4cm cap height would make it about 36 to 39cm wide, which overflows the 26cm artwork box. If the box size is kept, the cap height can only be about 2.9 to 3.1cm.
- On the back, the logo is never really there. The spec drops the maroon disc and every colour, so the seed only reads as burgundy fabric showing through. The only true version of the logo is a 5cm hem patch. Across a room, the back reads as 'a cream flame with eyes' plus the word, so the wordmark is doing all of the identifying.
- The art risk is still the layout. Even after the rebuild, it is a centred mascot with the name underneath, which is the default merch-back layout. The comic panel, the 'DAY 7' caption box and the tally help, but they add three insider devices. The result could read either as a sticker-box print or as a cluttered team-uniform logo, not as the illustration-led art the founder liked in Boy In A Dream. The founder has already rejected 'logo but bigger' kinds of safety.
- A large print area on the sweatiest part of the shirt: 35 to 45% coverage of 26 x 30cm on the upper back. If discharge fails and the fallback (two strokes of high-opacity water-based cream) is used, that patch on linen-cotton slub goes stiff, does not breathe, and cracks along the slubs after washing. Discharge on reactive burgundy often comes out pinkish-beige, not #EDE3CC, so 'cream' will probably need colour-discharge with pigment added. Also, few Bangkok printers will burn a screen and do discharge strike-offs for an 11-piece run without a minimum charge.
- Burgundy linen-cotton in 34C sun shows sweat marks at the back yoke and armpits. Reactive-dyed linen also fades unevenly with line drying. Within a few months that can look 'lived-in' or simply 'worn out', depending on the dye lot. Buying the exact #5E1B2C off the roll in Phahurat or Sampeng is unlikely; expect to take what is in stock.
- The front could get busy. Pocket, pen slot, glasses loop, 7 yellow stitches, jujizashi corner crosses, a hem patch, 5 coconut buttons, an edition tag and vents is a lot of 'story details' for a quiet front. Each is fine alone, but together they can tip into a concept-heavy, AI-lookbook feel.
- The costs are optimistic for a one-off run. 350 to 550 THB CMT for a camp shirt with French seams, a double yoke, vents, a loop and a pen slot is at the low end; small Pratunam workshops often quote 500 to 900 for 10 pieces. Plan on the top of the 14,000 to 27,000 THB range, or about 30,000.
- Teen wearability is plausible but not proven. Camp shirts are common in Bangkok streetwear and suit mentors well. Thai teens default to oversized tees, though, and a button-front burgundy linen shirt with a big back print is closer to 'cool older sibling' than to everyday school-adjacent wear. Check this with 2 or 3 students on the sample before cutting all 10.

### Fixes to the spec
- Rewrite the mascot tone map so detail survives the threshold. Set the flame base to about 55 to 62% ink, so it comes out as photocopy grain rather than solid cream. Set the bright yellow tongues to 100%, the orange tongues to about 30% (sparse grain), the eye whites to 100%, and the seed, pupils and disc to 0%. Knock out the brows (0% ink) wherever they sit over the flame and ink them only where they sit over the seed, or give each brow a 1-cell no-ink keyline on both sides. Extend the 1-cell keyline to the eye whites and to the 100% tongue edges. Render a proof at 1:1 and check that the tongues, both brows and both eyes are readable at 2m before burning the film.
- Fix the wordmark size against the box. At a 25cm width, the cap height is about 3.0cm, which at a 20 px cap height means a cell of about 1.5mm. That still holds on 110 mesh, as the cells stay at or above about 1.27mm. Measure the real advance width of the chosen OFL font, then set either the width or the cap height, not both.
- Add a 'logo legibility' check. Show the back proof to 3 people who do not know the brand and ask what it is. If no one reads 'flame / seed character', bring back a partial disc arc behind the mascot as the panel's background shape. The flame then visibly breaks a circle as well as the panel, which is the logo's own move.
- Make discharge the go/no-go gate rather than a preference. If the 20cm strike-off does not reach a cream that is soft after 3 washes, change the art, not the ink: cut coverage to 25 to 30% (more threshold grain, a thinner panel) and print one stroke of soft-hand water-based. Do not lay down two flashed strokes of high-opacity ink over 35 to 45% of a linen back.
- Pare down the front. Keep the pocket with its 7 yellow stitches, the coconut buttons and the hem patch. Drop either the pen slot or the jujizashi corner crosses, and hide the glasses loop inside the pocket edge rather than bar-tacking it under the pocket.
- Prototype the back art twice before committing, and let the founder pick: (A) the spec's panel with the threshold mascot and wordmark; (B) the same panel, but with an original black-ink comic scene drawn by a hired illustrator, for example the real-geometry seed at a night desk on Day 7. Price B at about 3,000 to 8,000 THB. B is the version most likely to feel like the 'art taste' the founder asked for, rather than a logo enlarged.
- Budget CMT at 500 to 900 THB and get 2 workshop quotes. Make the total about 18,000 to 30,000 THB. Decide before fabric purchase whether to keep codex-1's tee blank fallback with the same new back art, in black ink on undyed natural cotton. If the shirt workshop slips, that fallback is the cheapest route that still keeps the art.
- Fit and wear-test the sample on at least 2 teens and 1 mentor outdoors for half a day in Bangkok heat. Check sweat marks on the burgundy, how the print feels when damp, and whether teens would actually choose to wear it, before cutting 10.
